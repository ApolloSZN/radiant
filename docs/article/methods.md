# Methods note — America's hidden transformer-steel dependence

All figures are generated by `scripts/build_goes_article.py` from `radiant/data/goes_balance.py` and `radiant/data/goes_research.py`. Tests (`tests/test_goes_research.py`, `tests/test_goes_balance.py`, `tests/test_goes_article.py`) fail if the code, the data or this text drift apart. Phase memos with full source lists are in `data/goes/research/phase_0.md` to `phase_5.md`.

**Evidence types.** *Measured*: official statistics or audited filings. *Stated*: a company or agency claim (interested parties flagged). *Estimate*: a published estimate, or a derived estimate whose method is in code. *Model*: scenario output. *Secondhand*: known only via another report. Market-research figures are not used.

**Trade.** U.S.-reported annual trade, 2015–2025, by partner, from the UN Comtrade public API (reporter 842; `scripts/fetch_comtrade_goes.py`). GOES sheet is HS 7225.11 + 7226.11. Comtrade matches the Census API panel within 1% for exports and 5% for imports (general vs consumption basis) in 2019–2024. Exports are split into domestic exports and re-exports. Comtrade's weights for transformers (8504.21–.34) are imputed from value, so they are not used; a test enforces this. Core parts (8504.90) carry no weight at all.

**Sheet use and production, 2019.** Commerce's 2021 report ([FR 2021-24958](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2021-11-18/pdf/2021-24958.pdf)) gives sheet imports (27 kt), a sheet import share "less than 20 percent", an all-forms share "approximately 44 percent" with 68 kt in cores, and "over 50 percent" in 2020 with 96 kt. Solving these gives sheet use of 146–151 kt and all-forms use (sheet + cores) of 213–218 kt, matching the Core Coalition's 220 kt. Production = sheet use − imports + exports = 164–169 kt. A 2017 cross-check (37% share) gives 162 kt. No other year's production is identifiable. Other years use a *model* range from 162 kt to the stated ceiling (259 kt GOES-only, AK 2014, until 2019; 227 kt all electrical steel, Cliffs, from 2020).

**Cores.** Commerce's 68 kt (2019) is scaled by the value of Canadian + Mexican 8504.90 imports deflated by the U.S. GOES import unit value. The range is the 2019 level to that index (109 kt in 2025). 8504.90 includes non-core parts, and lamination unit counts fell in 2024 while this index rose.

**Finished transformers.** Large power transformers (>100 MVA): Commerce's 617 imports (2019) × 60–192 t GOES each, from DOE's 150–400 t unit weight and a 40% (DOE 2022) to 48% (NREL 2026) GOES share, giving 37–118 kt. After 2019 the floor is scaled by the real value of 8504.23 imports (BLS transformer PPI), and the ceiling is DOE's 900 units/yr × 82% imported × 192 t. Distribution transformers: GOES for all U.S. DTs (175 kt stakeholder estimate to 97% of DOE's 225 kt core steel) × the 2019 import unit share (17.7%), scaled by measured import units: 31–39 kt in 2019.

**Foreign share.** Domestic-origin GOES = production − domestic exports + U.S. steel that returns inside imported cores or transformers. That return is bounded from 0 up to domestic exports to Canada + Mexico. Foreign share = 1 − domestic-origin ÷ all-forms use. The low end of each range uses the most domestic combination, the high end the least.

**Checks.** 2019 production ≤ capacity. Sheet use from trade matches sheet use from Commerce's percentages within 3%. A bottom-up build by transformer class (DOE, MTC, Commerce unit counts) overlaps the top-down supply (Phase 3).

**Outlook (model).** Demand = 2019 all-forms use × NREL distribution-transformer capacity growth (1.6–3.4%/yr) + NREL transmission increment (94–114 kt/yr, phased in by 2030), minus DOE's 48 kt/yr amorphous shift from 2029 on the low side. Domestic supply = identified 2019 output, minus 2025-level exports, minus the DLA stockpile (to 2029); optionally +25% at Butler from 2028 (capped at 227 kt) and no exports. Results: 2035 demand 410–756 kt; foreign share 70–84% (no change), 49–72% (best case); never below 46% through 2035.

**Limits.** U.S. GOES output is never published. GOES content of 10–100 MVA transformers and non-transformer uses is unquantified. The 68 kt core anchor comes from an interested party. The outlook is a scenario model, not a forecast. No shortage, price or timing claim is made.
