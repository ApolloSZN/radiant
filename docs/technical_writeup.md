# Radiant: evidence-bounded models of adaptive systems

## Abstract

Radiant is an experimental research system for representing adaptive systems without collapsing topology, physical feasibility, forecasting, and causal claims into one vague model. The project began with a deliberately broad question: can one computational grammar describe systems ranging from autocatalytic chemistry to laboratories, industrial production, and technological capability development while remaining falsifiable? The answer so far is narrower than the original ambition, but more useful. Radiant now combines a typed adaptive-system kernel, topological RAF closure, quantitative stock-flow viability, bitemporal evidence, prequential forecasting, immutable forecast records, counterfactual traces, and constraint-resolution models. Every important claim is tagged by evidence strength. Illustrative networks are not treated as empirical proof. Retrospective time series are not treated as historical forecasts. Qualitative supply-chain evidence is not converted into numerical effect sizes.

The forecasting result that looked strongest turned out to be mostly a baseline effect. On a 47-point NHGRI sequencing-cost series, a causal prequential model selector reduces mean absolute log error from 0.7836 for an all-history log-linear baseline to 0.1762, a 77.5% reduction. However, a predeclared baseline-fairness audit shows the selector is not robustly better than a simple five-point rolling fit (9.6%, interval crossing zero) and is beaten by three- and four-point rolling fits. The defensible lesson is that recent data beats old data after a regime shift, not that the selector is a superior forecaster. A battery-price test is similarly weak: 0.1461 versus 0.1648 for a rolling baseline, an 11.4% reduction on six forecasts. A strict-vintage replay built from archived Bureau of Labor Statistics releases verifies that Radiant can operate with information genuinely available at each historical cutoff; its correction model loses to the baseline, and that negative result is retained.

## Why the architecture separates kinds of truth

A central design decision is that different questions require different tests. A reaction network may contain a reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated set while still being unable to maintain itself materially. Basic RAF theory is therefore implemented as a topological closure test, not a definition of life. A separate linear-programming viability layer represents inputs, outputs, process capacities, energy requirements, depreciation, maintenance, and replacement. A network is materially viable only if feasible positive flows cover the declared maintenance obligations over the modeled horizon.

The same separation applies higher in the stack. A time-series model that predicts well retrospectively has not necessarily passed a historical replay. A production dependency documented by a government report is not automatically the binding bottleneck. A factory expansion measured in dollars or jobs cannot be converted into transformers per year unless a source provides a defensible conversion. These distinctions are encoded in schemas, tests, and release gates rather than left to narrative caution.

Radiant uses evidence classes to preserve these boundaries. Measured values are distinct from identified bounds, justified probability distributions, and scenario assumptions. Bitemporal records track both the date an event or measurement describes and the date the evidence became available. This makes it possible to ask not only what is known now about 2015, but what could actually have been known at a cutoff in 2015.

## Forecasting as a falsification problem

The first real empirical spine was DNA sequencing cost. A simple all-history exponential trend performs poorly because the series contains a major technological regime shift associated with next-generation sequencing. A rolling-window model adapts faster. An early Radiant rule explicitly detected large innovations and shortened its memory after a break. That improved sequencing performance, but the same rule failed on lithium-ion battery pack prices: forced adaptation produced worse error than the stable all-history model.

Rather than hide that failure, Radiant changed the architecture. The current prequential selector maintains competing memory lengths and chooses the next model using only errors that have already been observed. The outcome being predicted cannot influence which model predicts it. A leakage test changes a future outcome by a factor of one hundred and verifies that the model choice made before that outcome remains unchanged.

On sequencing, the selector reaches 0.1762 MALE compared with 0.7836 for the all-history baseline. On the battery series it reaches 0.1461 compared with 0.1648 for the declared rolling baseline. These are real-data evaluations, but both dense datasets were assembled retrospectively. Radiant labels them `retrospective_real_data` and the release verifier refuses to interpret them as strict historical-vintage forecasts.

A later audit tested whether the headline depended on the choice of opponent. It does. The same selector was scored against a predeclared panel of non-adaptive baselines: all-history, rolling windows of 8, 6, 5, 4 and 3 points, and a no-change forecast. The selector robustly beats only all-history, rolling-8 and rolling-6. Against rolling-5 its improvement is 9.6% with a paired-bootstrap interval of -6.3% to 26.8%. Rolling-4 and rolling-3 beat it by 8% and 18%. The 77.5% figure is therefore a true statement about one comparison, not evidence of a forecasting advantage, and the evaluation report now carries the full panel.

Run 027 made that audit executable policy rather than a documentation caveat. The sequencing release row now fails unless the selector robustly beats every predeclared simple baseline, so the current scientific eval gate is red. Two causal online aggregation benchmarks were added to test whether a generic adaptive learner rescues the claim without target leakage: follow-the-leader reaches 0.1561 MALE and exponential weights 0.1641, both better than the original selector but still worse than the rolling-3 baseline at 0.1493. The project therefore preserves the negative conclusion rather than selecting a new formulation after seeing outcomes.

To exercise the harder information-time contract, Radiant includes a separate archive-derived dataset of preliminary and revised U.S. nonfarm-business productivity releases from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Each preliminary value has a publication date and a later revised value with its own publication date. At each preliminary release, a simple revision model can use only revisions that had already been published. Forecast payloads are hashed before the target revision is available. The current sample produces seven evaluable strict-vintage predictions. The purpose of this fixture is not to claim a breakthrough in macroeconomic forecasting; it is to prove that the machinery can replay a real historical information set without silently importing later revisions.

## Immutable forecasts and causal traces

Radiant treats a forecast as an artifact, not a mutable row. Registration records the cutoff, target, model specification, prediction, and evidence identifiers and generates a deterministic SHA-256 identity before the outcome exists. Resolution occurs later. Binary forecasts receive Brier and log scores; positive continuous forecasts receive log and factor error. Evidence ordering cannot change forecast identity, and backdated forecasts are rejected.

Counterfactuals also preserve intermediate mechanisms. A structural knockout does not merely report a final set of failed processes. It emits a causal directed acyclic graph linking removed resources to failed processes, newly unavailable intermediates, and downstream failures. This is still a model trace rather than proof of real-world causality, but it makes the model's claimed mechanism inspectable and testable.

## From supply-chain narratives to identified constraints

Large power transformers are Radiant's first real production-system case. Public U.S. evidence supports a topology involving grain-oriented electrical steel, continuously transposed copper conductors, insulation, core fabrication, winding, assembly, testing, qualification, and specialized factory equipment. The first solver maximizes declared transformer output subject to material and process-capacity constraints and then measures the marginal throughput effect of relaxing each constraint.

The first numerical transformer network used normalized scenario parameters. It demonstrated bottleneck migration: increasing one constrained stage can make a different stage bind, while increasing a widely discussed vulnerable input can produce zero marginal output when another stage is already limiting. Those numbers were explicitly retained as software validation, not estimates of the U.S. industry.

Subsequent work added uncertainty ensembles and distribution-free partial identification. When only bounds are justified, Radiant evaluates the admissible parameter region and certifies an intervention only when its ranking survives the region. A broad uncertainty stress test failed to identify one robust transformer bottleneck. That negative result is preferable to a crisp but unsupported ranking.

A stronger macro result is identifiable from public accounting. The 2019 evidence used by Radiant reports 137 domestically produced large power transformers, 617 imports, four exports, and about 40% domestic capacity utilization. Apparent consumption is therefore 750 units. Treating 137 as 40% of nameplate implies about 342.5 units per year of domestic nameplate capacity. Even the extreme counterfactual of fully utilizing that nameplate leaves roughly 411.5 units requiring external supply at the same demand level. The defensible claim is therefore structural: utilization of the reported 2019 domestic plant base alone could not have eliminated import dependence. Radiant does not infer from this why utilization was low.

Capacity announcements are handled with the same discipline. A disclosed unit-per-year addition can enter a capacity bound when product definitions are compatible. Investments, employment counts, square footage, and relative statements such as “double capacity” cannot be silently converted to absolute LPT output. Cross-definition arithmetic can be retained as a labeled scenario but not promoted to an identified result.

## Reproducibility and release discipline

Radiant's repository contains a deterministic test suite, CI evaluation harness, Dockerfile, one-command reproduction script, ADRs, limitations ledger, demo, migrations, datasets, and machine-readable run reports. The release verifier is fail-closed. It checks the evaluation artifact and CI configuration, clean-machine reproduction files, README, ADR count, limitations document, demo, technical-writeup word count, test artifact, and at least one passing strict-historical-vintage evaluation. Retrospective performance alone cannot satisfy the final evidence gate. After Run 027, the evaluation artifact itself is red because the sequencing selector fails the fair-baseline gate; GitHub Actions invokes the evaluator in strict mode so this cannot be hidden by local documentation.

This matters because scientific software can easily become persuasive architecture theater. A polished README is not evidence. A schema is not a measured system. A toy RAF is not proof of cross-scale universality. A Monte Carlo distribution is not justified merely because code can sample it. Radiant's release logic attempts to make these category errors mechanically difficult.

## What Radiant does not establish

Radiant does not establish a universal theory of living systems. It does not show that RAF closure is sufficient for self-maintenance. It does not prove that the same ontology survives every chemical, biological, industrial, and institutional domain without semantic adjustment. The current cross-scale examples outside the transformer and empirical time-series work remain illustrative.

The sequencing improvement is not a prospective forecast record and should not be described as one. The strict-vintage BLS fixture demonstrates information-time integrity on a small independent domain; it does not retroactively convert NHGRI data into contemporaneous observations. Battery results show that the strongest sequencing adaptation behavior does not generalize uniformly. Transformer evidence identifies some macro bounds and candidate constraints, but not the stage with the largest real marginal return to investment. A synthetic event-study harness exists for future expansion panels, but synthetic recovery is not causal evidence about factories.

## Current interpretation

The useful product of Radiant is increasingly not one grand equation. It is a disciplined computational contract for moving from evidence to claims. Topological closure asks whether enabling relationships exist. Quantitative viability asks whether rates and resources can sustain required stocks. Forecasting asks whether a model predicts under a declared information set. Counterfactual traces expose the mechanism assumed by a model. Constraint resolution asks what intervention changes feasible output. Partial identification asks whether that conclusion survives what is actually unknown.

The first adequacy result for grain-oriented electrical steel (GOES) is a conditional bound rather than a forecast. The sole U.S. producer states capacity of up to 250,000 short tons per year for all electrical steel, which bounds GOES capacity from above. Adding a DOE-funded laboratory's modeled transmission increment of 94 to 114 thousand tonnes to 2019 apparent consumption of roughly 220 thousand tonnes implies an import or stock-drawdown floor of 87 to 107 thousand tonnes per year, 3.2 to 4.0 times 2019 imports. The floor reaches zero only if non-grid use falls by 40 to 49 percent. A reported hot-mill expansion is kept qualitative because no GOES figure was stated. Radiant does not infer a shortage: imports may supply the gap.

That architecture has already rejected several tempting conclusions: that the first RAF is equivalent to life, that one adaptive forecasting rule generalizes across technology domains, that GOES vulnerability alone proves GOES is the binding transformer bottleneck, that workforce difficulty proves labor is the marginal constraint, or that announced investment can be translated directly into output.

The next scientific bottleneck is effect identification from real plant and procurement variation. The next useful evidence would connect order dates, specifications, vendors, quoted and realized delivery times, factory expansions, product mix, labor changes, test-space changes, material interruptions, qualification failures, and actual throughput. With such panels, Radiant's pre-registered pretrend and placebo checks can begin separating plausible mechanisms from measured effects.

Radiant v1.0 should therefore be read as a reproducible evidence-and-evaluation architecture with several real empirical demonstrations, not as a completed model of civilization. Its standard is narrower: every headline result must identify its baseline, information set, provenance, uncertainty class, and falsification conditions. Where the evidence cannot identify an answer, the system should say so and preserve exactly what measurement would reduce the uncertainty next.
