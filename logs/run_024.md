## Starting state

## Objective
Turn the NLR national GOES demand result into a falsifiable supply-adequacy statement without converting demand pressure into an unsupported shortage claim.

## Sources consulted
- NLR/DOE-funded 2026 transmission supply-chain study: modeled transmission expansion requires 94–114 kt GOES/year and this is more than 45% of average U.S. apparent consumption during 2019–2023.
- U.S. Department of Commerce Section 232 transformer/GOES report (published 2021): historical context includes approximately 220,000 metric tons/year U.S. GOES consumption and about 27,000 metric tons of GOES imports in 2019; source also documents concentration and product-grade constraints.
- Commerce SIMA documentation reviewed as a candidate authoritative trade-data path for a later vintage-aligned panel.

## Implementation
Added `SupplyPressureBound` and `nlr_goes_supply_pressure()` in `radiant/data/lpt_evidence_network.py`. The result preserves the source-identified inequality rather than solving backwards for a fabricated consumption point. It explicitly records that shortage is not identified.

## Quantitative outputs
- Modeled incremental GOES demand: 94–114 kt/year.
- Source-identified lower bound: incremental demand >45% of 2019–2023 average apparent consumption.
- Therefore, under a deliberately static counterfactual with no displacement, inventory drawdown, recycling response, imports/production expansion, total requirement index is >1.45 relative to the historical baseline.
- This is a pressure bound, not an adequacy deficit estimate.

## Failures / rejected approaches
- Rejected deriving historical apparent consumption as demand/0.45 because NLR says *more than* 45%, not exactly 45%.
- Rejected combining 2019 Commerce consumption with 2026 modeled demand as though it were a same-vintage future supply forecast.
- Rejected labeling the result a shortage because future domestic output, imports, inventories, substitution/displacement and competing uses are not jointly identified.
